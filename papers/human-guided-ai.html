Human-Guided Artificial Intelligence for Adaptive Compliance Automation in Financial Institutions

Abhiram Potharaju

Journal of Computational Analysis and Applications · 35(4), 57–68 · 2026-04-06

Journal publication · 2026Original published work

Abstract

With the increasing pressure on financial institutions to automate regulatory compliance while keeping the auditing and transparency of the decision-making process, the Adaptive Compliance Intelligence System illustrates how human-artificial intelligence architectures at multiple layers can be orchestrated using event-driven microservices to overcome the challenges of scaling, interpretability, and accountability of regulatory compliance automation. Current rule systems are brittle and difficult to maintain, while standard black-box ML models tend to be accurate but not interpretable enough for high-stakes regulatory applications. The proposed framework consists of confidence-weighted routing models that route cases down different processing paths based on model confidence, regulatory complexity of the predicted outputs, and certain transaction features. Architectural improvements, including the scaling of analytical functions and implementations based on containerized microservices, allow for fine-tuning based on computing needs. Human-in-the-loop experiments track human oversight for validation, augmentation, and overrides, feeding the results back into the continuous learning pipeline to improve model calibration and reduce false positives over time. In healthcare applications, intelligible models match or outperform opaque neural nets. In addition, these intelligible models enable domain experts to identify and correct spurious correlations that lead to harmful decisions. Use of the framework reduces the time and effort of auditing by orders of magnitude, while achieving the same decision accuracy, because human expertise is exercised on cases where it can add judgment and context rather than pattern matching. Adoption rates for cloud-native systems show that the distributed architecture is now the norm in financial services and almost universal in container orchestration platforms. The point is that machine learning for performance tuning is the difference between instant and wide-ranging human effort. Algorithm changes result in an operational change. The development of compliance technology has been shaped by the intersection of computation and human accountability, balancing progress against risk in heavily regulated environments.
